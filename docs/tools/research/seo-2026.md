# Google SEO for free tool pages: late-2026 best practice

Research for the 7 free tool pages on usegeo.app. Written 11 October 2026.

How this was checked: Google Search Central docs, blog and changelog pages were read in full on 11 Oct 2026 (through the Treg page reader, because direct fetches of developers.google.com were blocked here). Studies were read on the publisher's own page where possible. Anything taken only from secondary coverage says so. Anything not confirmed is marked **UNVERIFIED**. No numbers below are invented: each one has a source. Paid calls for this research cost about US$0.06 in total.

---

## Key takeaways (read this if nothing else)

1. **The tool is the content.** Google's own docs now list "calculators, online tools" as main content and rate pages on effort, originality and "functional page tools". Put the working tool at the top, server-rendered, no signup.
2. **FAQ rich results are gone.** Google stopped showing them on **7 May 2026** and removed the docs in June 2026. HowTo has been dead since Sept 2023. FAQ answers on the page still matter (people and AI read them); the FAQPage markup is now optional.
3. **SoftwareApplication / WebApplication rich results need a real rating or review.** We have none, so free tools won't get a rich result. Never fake ratings.
4. **One canonical host.** usegeo.app must be the only indexable host. Redirect public pages on the railway.app host, or at least point every canonical at usegeo.app. Don't combine noindex with a cross-host canonical.
5. **Seven genuinely different tools is fine. Seventy keyword variants is scaled content abuse.** Keep one page per real tool, and make tool result URLs noindex.
6. **All 7 target searches show a Google AI Overview today** (our live check, 11 Oct 2026), and those AI Overviews cite tool pages by name. Being cited is the realistic goal: Seer measured +120% organic clicks per impression for cited brands vs not cited on the same results page.
7. **New in Search Console (all sites since 31 Aug 2026):** a Generative AI performance report and a "Search generative AI" control. Check the control is on "Include".

### 1.2 What Google rewards on a tool page (primary sources)

From "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content, read 11 Oct 2026):

- **Main content explicitly includes tools:** "Interactive features: Built-in functionality such as calculators, online tools, games, or search functionality."
- **Rater criteria for main content:** *Effort* ("building a custom interactive map ... represents high effort"; "using generative AI to produce large amounts of text without manual oversight ... represents little to no effort"), *Originality*, *Talent or skill* ("clear writing, well-produced video, or functional page tools"), *Accuracy*.
- **Who, How, Why:** say who made it (bylines "where one might be expected", leading to more about the author); explain how it works (Google: "Sharing details about the processes involved can help readers ... understand any unique and useful role automation may have served"); the why must be "primarily to help people".
- **Fake authors are a negative signal:** "Fabricating creator profiles (such as by using AI-generated headshots, made-up names, or false credentials ...) is a form of deception."
- **Word count:** "Are you writing to a particular word count because you've heard or read that Google has a preferred word count? (No, we don't.)"
- **Fake freshness is a warning sign:** "Are you changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?"

From "Optimizing for generative AI features" (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide, added 15 May 2026, last updated 2026-07-10):

- SEO best practice still applies to AI Overviews and AI Mode because they "are rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems" (retrieval-augmented generation plus "query fan-out").
- Make "non-commodity content": a unique point of view, not "what others on the internet have already said, or could easily be produced by a generative AI model".
- **Explicit warning:** creating separate content "for every possible variation of how people might search (for example ... fan-out queries)" to manipulate rankings or AI responses "violates Google's scaled content abuse spam policy".
- To appear in AI features a page "must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet". Do not use `nosnippet` on tool pages.
- Things you can ignore for Google: llms.txt and "special" markup ("Google Search itself doesn't use them"), "chunking" content, rewriting content just for AI, inauthentic mentions, and over-focusing on structured data ("there's no special schema.org markup you need to add").
- Agents: browser agents read screenshots, the DOM and the accessibility tree; Google points to web.dev's agent-friendly UX guide (see section 5).

### 1.3 Spam policies that touch a free-tool set (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies, read 11 Oct 2026)

| Policy | What it says | What it means for our 7 tools |
|---|---|---|
| **Scaled content abuse** | "many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users ... no matter how it's created" | One page per genuinely different tool. Do NOT make separate near-copy pages for "llms.txt checker", "llms.txt validator", "llms.txt generator", or per-platform clones ("for Shopify", "for WooCommerce") with the same tool. Cover close variants on one page. Never let tool results create indexable pages at scale. |
| **Doorway abuse** | includes "Generating pages to funnel visitors into the actual usable or relevant portion of a site" | The tool must fully work on the page with no signup. One CTA under the result is fine; a page that only teases and pushes to the app is not. |
| **Misleading functionality** | sites that "claim to provide certain functionality ... but intentionally" don't | Each tool must do what its title says. The "AI bot checker" page must say plainly, above the fold, that it checks whether product copy reads like generic bot text and that no tool can prove who wrote text. |
| **Machine-generated traffic** | "scraping results for rank-checking purposes or other types of automated access to Google Search conducted without express permission" violates the spam policies and Google's Terms | The AI Overview checker queries Google through a third-party data provider. This is common across SEO tools (seo.com, Semrush and Ahrefs run AI Overview checkers that Google's own AI Overview cites), and we found no documented case of Google demoting the site that shows such data (**UNVERIFIED**). Ross should know the policy text exists. Cache results and rate-limit. |
| **Malicious practices: back button hijacking** (new, 13 Apr 2026) | interfering with the back button by "manipulating the browser history" | When a tool writes its state to the URL, use `history.replaceState`, never stack `pushState` entries; never trap the back button after a result. |
| **Site reputation** (updated 28 Aug 2026 for the EEA) | about third-party content riding a host's reputation | Not an issue for first-party tools, but note this line: "Google generally applies a presumption that individual pages (including new pages) match the overall quality of other pages on the domain." Weak tool pages can drag the whole domain, and vice versa. |
| **Spam policies apply to AI answers** (clarified 15 May 2026) | the policies cover "attempting to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search" | Don't add hidden text or prompt-style text aimed at AI systems. |

### 1.4 What makes tool pages rank (synthesis)

From the sources above plus the page-1 results we looked at:

- **Working tool above the fold on a phone**, server-rendered, no signup, fast result. Google calls the tool main content, and AI Overviews for these searches describe and cite tool pages (section 7).
- **Unique value beyond the 8 to 10 near-identical competitors.** Our edge is Shopify-specific results and plain-English fixes ("where to click in Shopify admin"). The `app/lib/robots.ts` notes (for example, that Shopify's default robots.txt blocks no AI bot) are the kind of first-hand, non-commodity facts Google asks for. Only state facts we have checked.
- **Honesty where rivals overclaim.** Kedra's FAQ generator page (title "Free FAQ Schema Generator (JSON-LD)", about 810 words, read 11 Oct 2026) still has a section "Why it helps in Google and in AI answers". Since Google dropped FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026, an accurate page is both more helpful and a real difference.
- **E-E-A-T signals that are true:** visible "Made by GEO" with a link to an About page (a real person's name and role if Ross agrees: ask him, never invent one), a "How this works" section naming the data source, and a visible "Last updated" date that only changes when the tool or text changes.
- **Weak spots in competitors:** one page-1 result for "llms.txt checker" (llmsvalidator.com) now serves a parked "Redirecting..." page (11 Oct 2026). Page-1 tool pages we measured run about 240 to 940 words (ghostwall.co AI crawler checker 238; Kedra FAQ generator 810; SEOptimer schema tester 896; seo.com AI Overview checker 939). This is what currently ranks, not a target Google sets.

## 2. On-page and site structure

### 2.1 Title tag (Title links doc, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link, read 11 Oct 2026)

- Every page needs a `<title>`; "descriptive and concise"; no keyword stuffing; no boilerplate repeated across pages.
- Google truncates "typically to fit the device width" and sets no length limit. Industry habit is to keep the important part within about 50 to 60 characters (**industry norm, not a Google rule**).
- Brand once, at the end, with a delimiter ("hyphen, colon, or pipe"). Our house rule bans em dashes, so use a colon or pipe.
- Google builds the title link from the `<title>`, the main visual title, `<h1>`, `og:title`, prominent text, anchor text and WebSite structured data. Keep `<title>`, H1 and `og:title` consistent so Google has no reason to rewrite.
- Pattern: `{Tool name}: {plain benefit} | GEO`, for example `AI Crawler Checker: See Which AI Bots Can Read Your Site | GEO`.

### 2.2 Meta description (Snippets doc, https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet, read 11 Oct 2026)

- Snippets are "primarily created from the page content itself"; Google uses the meta description only "if it might give users a more accurate description". Rewrites are normal and vary by query.
- Must be unique per page; no keyword lists; no length limit, truncated to device width (industry habit about 150 to 160 characters, **industry norm**).
- Say what the tool does, that it is free with no sign-up, and how long it takes.
- **"Read more" deep links** (added 20 Apr 2026): content should be "immediately visible on the page" (not hidden in tabs or accordions), don't force scroll position on load, and don't strip the `#fragment` from the URL. So show FAQ answers open, not collapsed.

### 2.3 H1, headings, intro, length

- One visible `<h1>` that matches the title's main term ("AI crawler checker"). Google: make the main title "the first visible `<h1>`" and visually dominant, or it may pick another heading.
- Directly under the H1: a one or two sentence plain-English answer of what the tool does and for whom (25 to 50 words), then the input. AI Overviews for these searches open with exactly that kind of definition ("An AI crawler checker is a diagnostic tool that tests whether ...", live check 11 Oct 2026).
- Below the result: "What the results mean", "How to fix it in Shopify", "How this works" (data source, limits), FAQs (4 to 6), "Last updated" and who made it, then related tools. Use H2 for sections, H3 for each FAQ question.
- Length: Google has no preferred word count. Write what a store owner needs and stop; current page-1 tools sit between about 240 and 940 words.

### 2.4 URL slugs

- Short, lowercase, hyphenated, matching the head term, under one hub: `/tools/ai-overview-checker`, `/tools/ai-crawler-checker`, `/tools/product-schema-checker`, `/tools/faq-schema-generator`, `/tools/llms-txt-checker`, `/tools/ai-bot-checker`, `/tools/ai-visibility-checker`, hub at `/tools`. (Google's URL structure doc was revamped 18 Jun 2025 and still favours simple, readable words; docs-only change.)
- Pick one trailing-slash style (recommend none) and 301 the other. No query strings in canonical URLs.

### 2.5 Internal linking, hub, footer, breadcrumbs

- **Hub page** `/tools`: one card per tool (name, one-line job, link). Linked from the home page header or footer.
- **Every tool page** links to the hub and to all 6 sibling tools (a "More free tools" block plus the shared footer). Use descriptive anchor text (the tool name), plain `<a href>` links that work without JavaScript.
- **Footer on the home page and every tool page** lists all 7 tools and the hub. Internal footer links are normal; the link-spam policy's footer clause is about links spread across *other* sites' footers.
- Link to canonical URLs only (Google: "link to the canonical URL rather than a duplicate URL").
- **Breadcrumbs:** visible "Home > Free tools > {Tool}" plus BreadcrumbList markup. Google stopped showing breadcrumbs in **mobile** results in January 2025 and still shows them on desktop (**secondary sources only**: Sitebulb guide, Bruce Clay; Google's post was titled "Simplifying the visible URL element on mobile search results"). BreadcrumbList remains a supported feature in Google's structured data gallery (nav list, read 11 Oct 2026).
- **Single source of truth:** generate the hub, footer list, sibling links and sitemap from one tool registry so they can't drift.

### 2.6 Canonical tags (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls, read 11 Oct 2026)

- Self-referencing `rel="canonical"` on every tool page, **absolute** URL (`https://usegeo.app/tools/...`), in the server HTML `<head>`.
- Don't let JavaScript change it (Dec 2025 clarification: canonicalization "happens before and after rendering"; set it in the HTML source and "make sure that JavaScript doesn't change" it).
- Don't use robots.txt for canonicalization; don't send conflicting canonicals (sitemap says A, tag says B).
- Google: "We don't recommend using noindex to prevent selection of a canonical page within a single site ... rel=canonical link annotations are the preferred solution."
- Canonical changes can take a while to be re-evaluated (canonicalization troubleshooting guide updated 10 Jul 2026).

### 2.7 The duplicate railway.app host

The same app answers on `https://geo-shopify-app-production.up.railway.app` and on `https://usegeo.app`. Search engines and AI systems should only ever see usegeo.app for public pages.

Google's options, strongest first: redirects ("Use permanent redirects ... when deprecating a duplicate page"), then rel=canonical (works across domains), then sitemaps (weakest). Bing (blog, 19 Dec 2025, https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2025/12/Does-Duplicate-Content-Hurt-SEO-and-AI-Search-Visibility/): duplicates "dilute authority", and "LLMs group near-duplicate URLs into a single cluster and then choose one page to represent the set", which may be the wrong one.

Recommendation:

1. **Best:** on the railway host, 301 every public GET page (`/`, `/check`, `/tools`, `/tools/*`, `/privacy`, `/tips`, `/sitemap.xml`, `/llms.txt`) to the same path on `https://usegeo.app`. Leave Shopify paths alone (`/app/*`, `/auth/*`, `/webhooks/*`, `/api/*`, `/healthz`), because `shopify.app.toml` points at the Railway URL (CLAUDE.md §18).
2. **Minimum (do this either way):** every public page emits `<link rel="canonical" href="https://usegeo.app/...">` regardless of which host served it, and `og:url` uses usegeo.app too. Sitemap and robots.txt `Sitemap:` line list usegeo.app URLs only.
3. **Don't** pair a cross-host canonical with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on the railway host. The signals conflict; Google staff have said noindex can be carried over to the canonical target in that case (**UNVERIFIED**: from past Google office-hours remarks, not in current docs). If redirects are impossible and canonicals alone are not enough, use noindex alone on the railway host's public pages, not both.
4. Don't block the railway host in robots.txt: Google then can't see the redirect or canonical.
5. Google's site-names doc: if duplicate home pages exist, "make sure that you're using the same structured data on all page duplicates".

### 2.8 Result pages and query-string states (our own risk)

- `/check/:id` result pages and any tool URL with a query string (for example `?site=nike.com`) must send `noindex` (meta robots, or `X-Robots-Tag`) and stay out of the sitemap. Otherwise anyone can mint thousands of thin pages on our domain, which looks like scaled content and risks the domain-wide "presumption" (1.3). noindex does not stop social previews, so shared results still get an OG card.
- Prefer POST (or a client-side fetch) for running a tool, so result URLs aren't crawlable by default. If a GET result URL exists for sharing, noindex it and canonicalise to the clean tool URL.
- The embedded admin (`/app/*`) is behind login; also send `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` there.

### 2.9 /check vs /tools/ai-visibility-checker (decision for Ross)

Both would target the same intent with the same check. Two indexable pages for one intent compete, and Bing says AI systems may pick the wrong one of a near-duplicate pair. Pick one indexable URL: either 301 `/check` to the tool page (if no ad or link depends on `/check`), or keep `/check` working with `rel=canonical` to `/tools/ai-visibility-checker`. Don't index both with the same copy.

### 3.2 Does FAQPage markup still have any value?

- **Google:** none visible. Google's long-standing line: "Structured data that's not being used does not cause problems for Search, but also has no visible effects" (8 Aug 2023). The AI optimization guide says structured data "isn't required for generative AI search".
- **Bing / Copilot:** Microsoft's Fabrice Canel said at SMX Munich (March 2025) that schema markup helps Microsoft's LLMs understand content (**secondary**: Search Engine Land, https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-bing-copilot-use-schema-for-its-llms-453455). No Microsoft doc ties FAQPage to Copilot citations.
- **Controlled evidence:** Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD (Aug 2025 to Mar 2026) against about 4,000 matched controls: AI Overviews citations −4.6% (significant but likely not caused by schema), AI Mode +2.4% and ChatGPT +2.2% ("statistically indistinguishable from zero"). https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/ (May 2026; publish date 11 May per secondary coverage). Their earlier 6M-URL correlation (cited pages about 3x more likely to have JSON-LD) is correlation only.
- **Verdict:** the visible FAQ text is what matters. FAQPage JSON-LD is cheap and harmless, so add it only if it mirrors the visible Q&A word for word. Don't promise anyone that it helps Google. This is also the honest message for the FAQ schema generator page itself.

## 4. robots.txt, sitemap, IndexNow, Search Console

### 4.1 robots.txt for usegeo.app

Observed 11 Oct 2026 (read-only): `public/` has no robots.txt and there is no sitemap route yet.

```
User-agent: *
Allow: /
Disallow: /app/

Sitemap: https://usegeo.app/sitemap.xml
```

- Don't block AI crawlers (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended). We sell AI visibility, and visitors will run our own crawler checker on usegeo.app.
- robots.txt doesn't stop indexing ("Google may still index URLs that are disallowed in robots.txt without their content"). Use noindex for pages that must stay out (result pages), and don't also disallow them, or Google never sees the noindex.
- Google reads the first 500 KiB of robots.txt (already noted in `app/lib/robots.server.ts`).

### 4.2 XML sitemap (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap, read 11 Oct 2026)

- "Google ignores `<priority>` and `<changefreq>` values." Bing also ignores them (Bing blog, 31 Jul 2025, https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2025/7/Keeping-Content-Discoverable-with-Sitemaps-in-AI-Powered-Search/).
- "Google uses the `<lastmod>` value if it's consistently and verifiably ... accurate." Bing: use full ISO 8601 date and time, and "Avoid setting lastmod to the time your sitemap was generated unless the content on that URL was actually updated."
- Absolute canonical URLs only, UTF-8, at the root. List: `/`, `/check` (unless canonicalised away, see 2.9), `/tools`, the 7 tools, `/privacy`, and any public guide pages. Never list result pages, query-string URLs or railway.app URLs.
- Keep each tool's `lastmod` in the tool registry, changed by hand when the tool or copy really changes (same date as the visible "Last updated" and `dateModified`).
- Submit in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and add the `Sitemap:` line to robots.txt. Bing fetches a submitted sitemap immediately and then "at least once per day".

### 4.3 IndexNow (Bing and others)

- Spec: https://www.indexnow.org/documentation (read 11 Oct 2026). Host a key file `https://usegeo.app/{key}.txt` (8 to 128 chars of a-z, A-Z, 0-9, dash) containing the key; POST JSON `{host, key, urlList}` (up to 10,000 URLs) to a participating engine's `/indexnow` endpoint whenever a page is added, changed or removed. 200 = received, 202 = key validation pending.
- Bing pushes it for AI freshness ("combining sitemaps and real-time URL submission methods like IndexNow", Bing blog 31 Jul 2025); Canel also said generative AI values fresh content and suggested IndexNow (secondary, SMX Munich 2025).
- Google does not use IndexNow; for Google, rely on the sitemap and Search Console (**UNVERIFIED** for 2026: Google's docs read here never mention IndexNow).
- Practical: on each deploy that changes tool pages, ping the changed URLs once. Don't ping on every request or for unchanged pages.

### 4.4 Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools steps for the new pages

1. Search Console: add a **Domain property** for `usegeo.app` (DNS TXT record). It covers http/https and all subdomains.
2. Settings > **Search generative AI**: confirm "Include my site's links and content in Search generative AI features" (the default; rolled out to all sites 31 Aug 2026; "Exclude" removes us from AI Overviews, AI Mode and Discover AI features). https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024
3. Sitemaps report: submit `https://usegeo.app/sitemap.xml`.

## 5. Speed, rendering, mobile and accessibility

### 5.1 Core Web Vitals (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals, last updated 2025-12-10)

- **LCP** within 2.5 s, **INP** under 200 ms, **CLS** under 0.1. (Measured at the 75th percentile of real visits per web.dev; **from memory, not re-checked today**.)
- Google ranks on overall page experience, not one or two metrics ("should not focus on only one or two aspects").
- Tool-specific traps: the result panel appearing and pushing the page down (CLS: reserve its space or render it below the fold of the input); long progress animations on the main thread (INP); a big hero image as LCP (make the H1 text the LCP element).
- **Server location:** the Railway service runs in Singapore (CLAUDE.md §18). US searchers (for example 260 of 590 monthly searches for "ai overview checker") pay the round trip on every HTML request, which hurts LCP. Put a CDN in front of the public pages and cache the static tool-page HTML at the edge, or move/replicate the public web service closer to the US (**our inference**, not measured).

### 5.2 Server-side rendering and JavaScript

- Title, meta description, canonical, robots meta, OG tags, H1, intro, how-it-works text, FAQs, breadcrumbs, footer links and JSON-LD must all be in the first HTML response (React Router `meta`/`links`/loader output). Google renders JavaScript (JS SEO doc, changelog 4 Mar 2026: "using JavaScript to load content is not 'making it harder for Google Search'"), but other crawlers and many AI fetchers don't run JS, and the house rule says server-render.
- Make the tool work without JavaScript where possible: a real `<form method="post">` that the server answers (React Router `<Form>` enhances it). Then the tool works for crawlers, agents and slow phones.
- Googlebot only reads "the first 2MB" of an HTML file, uncompressed, and each JS/CSS file has the same cap (Googlebot doc, Feb 2026). Keep inline hydration data small.
- Don't rely on JS to remove a `noindex` (Dec 2025: "If there's a possibility that you do want the page indexed, don't use a noindex tag in the original page code"). Pages with non-200 status may not be rendered (Dec 2025).
- Keep tool-page JS light: no chart or animation libraries on first load, lazy-load result-only components, self-host or preload fonts with `font-display: swap`.

### 5.3 Mobile-first

- Since 5 July 2024 Google crawls essentially all sites with its smartphone crawler (Search Central blog, 3 Jun 2024; seen via localized copies and Search Engine Journal). The mobile page is the page Google indexes: same content, links and structured data on mobile.
- Input above the fold at 360 px width; `type="url"` (or `inputmode="url"`), `autocapitalize="off"`, `autocorrect="off"`, `spellcheck="false"`; input text at least 16 px so iOS doesn't zoom (**common practice, UNVERIFIED** today); tap targets roomy; no horizontal scroll.

### 5.4 Accessibility and agent-readiness (web.dev "agent-friendly website" guide, linked from Google's AI optimization guide: https://web.dev/articles/ai-agent-site-ux)

- Semantic HTML: real `<button>` and `<a>`, not clickable `<div>`s; `<label for>` on every input; one H1, ordered headings.
- No "ghost elements" (transparent overlays over controls); stable layout; interactive elements visible and not tiny.
- Results in an `aria-live="polite"` region; visible focus states; colour contrast at WCAG AA (4.5:1 for body text); respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- These help screen readers, Google's agents and other AI agents at the same time.

### 5.5 Interstitials

- No email popup or modal on load or over the tool. Google's page experience guidance asks for content that is easy to tell apart and no intrusive interstitials (Google's "Interstitials and dialogs" doc, nav read 11 Oct 2026; content **from memory**). Offer email capture only after a result, and only when the visitor asks.

```html
<meta property="og:title" content="AI Crawler Checker: See Which AI Bots Can Read Your Site">

<meta property="og:image" content="https://usegeo.app/og/ai-crawler-checker.png">
<meta property="og:image:width" content="1200">
<meta property="og:image:height" content="630">
<meta property="og:image:alt" content="AI crawler checker result showing which bots are allowed">
<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image">
```

- **Size:** 1200 x 630 px PNG or JPG, under 5 MB. Facebook (doc updated 30 Jun 2026): use "at least 1200 x 630 pixels", minimum 600 x 315 for the large card, keep close to 1.91:1, max 8 MB, and set `og:image:width`/`height` so the first share renders. X `summary_large_image` (legacy Twitter docs): 2:1 aspect, minimum 300 x 157, maximum 4096 x 4096, under 5 MB, JPG/PNG/WEBP/GIF, no SVG. 1200 x 630 works for both (X crops slightly).
- **Google also uses `og:image`** for Search and Discover thumbnails (2 Mar 2026). Google's advice: relevant and representative, high resolution, no extreme aspect ratio, and avoid "a generic image (for example, your site logo) or an image with text". So make each tool's image a clean picture of that tool's result with little or no text, not the logo.
- Replacing an image: use a new image URL (Facebook caches by URL).
- X falls back to `og:` tags for title, description and image when `twitter:` tags are missing (**from memory, UNVERIFIED** today), so `twitter:card` is the only must-have extra.
- Result pages (noindex) still get OG tags so shared results look good.

### 7.3 What this means for our tool pages

- Even "checker" and "generator" searches get an AI Overview, and the overview answers "what is it" and lists tools. It cannot run the check on the searcher's own store, so a reason to click remains: the tool must deliver that personal result fast.
- Most cited URLs are the tool pages themselves, often small sites. Citations are winnable; Seer's +120% click lift for cited vs uncited is the prize.
- The overviews follow a pattern: a one-sentence definition, then "What X checks" bullets, then named popular tools. Mirror that in visible HTML near the top: a crisp definition, a short "What this checks" list, and accurate specifics (bot names, file paths, Shopify steps).
- "ai bot checker" is owned by AI-text detectors. Our honest copy-quality angle is unlikely to rank for the head term; judge it on the copy-quality searches it can win and on its usefulness to merchants.
- "product schema checker": the overview sends people to Google's own tools first. Differentiate on the AI angle (which product facts AI assistants can read) and Shopify fixes.
- Measure with the Search Console Generative AI performance report and Bing AI Performance, not just rankings.

## CHECKLIST: apply to every tool page

**Content and trust**
- [ ] Working tool is the first thing under the H1 at 360 px width; no signup; one result; exactly one CTA button under the result.
- [ ] One visible H1 matching the main term; a 25 to 50 word plain-English definition under it.
- [ ] Sections below the result: What the results mean, How to fix it in Shopify, How this works (data source and limits), 4 to 6 FAQs (open, not collapsed), Last updated + Made by GEO (link to About; real name only if Ross agrees).
- [ ] Copy written or reviewed by a human; no invented numbers, reviews or authors; no em dashes; no "GEO/AEO/LLM" jargon except where a search term needs it (llms.txt).
- [ ] Honest limits stated (FAQ rich results gone since 7 May 2026; llms.txt not used by Google Search; no tool can prove who wrote text; AI answers vary per search).

**Head tags (server-rendered, absolute usegeo.app URLs)**
- [ ] Unique `<title>`: `{Tool}: {benefit} | GEO`, key part within about 60 characters.
- [ ] Unique meta description: what it does, free, no sign-up, how long.
- [ ] Self-referencing absolute canonical to `https://usegeo.app/tools/{slug}`, never changed by JS.
- [ ] `<html lang="en">`, viewport meta, favicon.
- [ ] OG tags + `twitter:card=summary_large_image`, 1200 x 630 tool-specific image with little or no text, width/height/alt set.
- [ ] JSON-LD `@graph`: WebApplication (price 0, no ratings) + WebPage (`dateModified`, `primaryImageOfPage`) + BreadcrumbList. Optional FAQPage only if word-for-word identical to the visible FAQs.

**Links and structure**
- [ ] URL `/tools/{slug}`, lowercase, hyphens, no trailing slash, no query string.
- [ ] Visible breadcrumb Home > Free tools > Tool.
- [ ] "More free tools" block linking all 6 siblings + hub, and the shared footer listing all 7 tools on home and every tool page, all plain `<a href>`.
- [ ] Hub, footer, siblings and sitemap generated from one tool registry.

**Indexing**
- [ ] 200 status; in `sitemap.xml` with an accurate ISO 8601 `lastmod` (no priority/changefreq).
- [ ] Result pages, query-string URLs and `/app/*` send noindex and are not in the sitemap; tool runs use POST or client fetch.
- [ ] Public pages on the railway.app host 301 to usegeo.app (or, at minimum, canonical to usegeo.app); never noindex + canonical together.
- [ ] robots.txt allows all public paths and AI crawlers, lists the sitemap.
- [ ] After deploy: URL Inspection + Request indexing in Search Console; IndexNow ping for changed URLs; check the Search generative AI control is "Include".

**Speed and usability**
- [ ] LCP under 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms, CLS under 0.1 on a mid-range phone; H1 text is the LCP element; result area space reserved.
- [ ] Works without JavaScript (plain form post) where the tool allows; JS bundle for the page kept small; HTML well under 2 MB.
- [ ] Edge caching or a closer region for US visitors (server is in Singapore).
- [ ] Labels on inputs, real buttons, aria-live results, AA contrast, focus states, reduced motion respected.
- [ ] No popup on load; email capture only after a result, on request; `replaceState` (not `pushState` stacks) for URL state; don't strip `#fragments`.

## Things NOT to do

- Don't create near-duplicate pages per keyword variant or per platform with the same tool (scaled content abuse, doorway).
- Don't let tool results or shared result URLs become indexable pages.
- Don't add `aggregateRating` or `review` without genuine, visible reviews; never fake reviews, authors, headshots or credentials.
- Don't use HowTo markup; don't tell users FAQ schema gets them Google rich results.
- Don't hide the tool behind signup, an email gate or a popup; don't make the page a funnel to the app.
- Don't title a tool for something it doesn't do (misleading functionality), especially "AI bot checker".
- Don't bump "Last updated", `dateModified` or `lastmod` without a real change.
- Don't put `nosnippet` or `max-snippet:0` on tool pages (it removes eligibility for AI features).
- Don't block AI crawlers or block the railway host in robots.txt; don't pair noindex with a cross-host canonical.
- Don't let JavaScript set or change the canonical, title or robots meta; don't stack history entries or hijack the back button.
- Don't add hidden text or instructions aimed at AI systems; spam policies cover AI answers too.
