# Competitor ads map: how AI visibility tools advertise (checked 10 Oct 2026)

Purpose: see how competitors sell "get recommended by AI" so GEO's ads stand apart.
Everything below was opened and read on 10 Oct 2026. Ad quotes are verbatim, except where a
competitor used a dash: those spots are cut with "..." so this file keeps the house rule.

## How this was researched (and the limits)

- **Meta Ad Library.** The Facebook Ads tool (`ads_library_search`) worked, but it only returns page
  name, link headline and dates, not the ad text. So the full ad text, CTA, format and dates came from
  the Meta Ad Library via Treg (ScrapeCreators endpoints `facebook-adlibrary-company-ads` and
  `facebook-adlibrary-search-ads`). Every ad has a public link: `https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=<id>`.
- **"Running since"** = first delivery date in the Ad Library. **Active** = still delivering on 9 Oct 2026.
  Days are counted to 10 Oct 2026. An ad (or one headline across many variants) that runs 60+ days is
  usually one that works.
- **App Store listings and homepages** were read with a page scraper (Treg `anyapi.web.scrape`).
- Not found on Meta (searched by name and by page): **AgentIQ by 40rty, Kedra, Mento, DeepLumen,
  AthenaHQ, mentions.so**. They may advertise on Google or the Shopify App Store instead (not checked).
- Ad Library shows ads, not results. Spend and performance are not public.

### Format notes (what the long-runners look like)

- **StoreRank** (most persistent Shopify-app advertiser): one headline (a fear stat) for nearly a year,
  tested across static image, video and dynamic formats. Long-form primary text: news hook, "Sounds great, right? Wrong.",
  checklist of features, trial CTA.
- **Otterly**: short video, one bold claim as the headline ("Rank #1 on ChatGPT & Co."), no body copy. Ran 8 months.
- **Era Search**: a do-it-yourself test in the first line. Still running after 172 days.
- **Profound / Peec / Goodie**: research-stat openers ("We analyzed 6.1 million AI citations", "Based on 232,000
  citations"). Peec tested 8 such headlines at once for 1 to 4 days each.
- **Scrunch**: lead-gen form with a $100 gift card to book a demo. That signals an expensive enterprise sale,
  not a self-serve merchant product.

## 5. Five takeaways for GEO's ads

1. **Own the money, in the ad itself (once it works on real stores).** Revenue tracking is fast becoming a standard
   feature on the App Store (AgentIQ, StoreRank, DeepLumen, Kedra, TinyAI, Sonari), so "we track revenue" alone won't
   stand out, and "other tools only count mentions" would be false. What we found no one showing in an ad is the
   merchant's own orders from AI against their baseline, as growth since joining. Lead with that screen, never call
   it "the only", and re-check competitors during the campaign.
2. **The ad-cost angle is unclaimed here.** No AI visibility tool uses it, while neighbouring categories show it lands
   with Shopify owners. Use the true version of Ross's line, not "ad costs only go one way" (false: Meta's price per ad
   fell for 7 quarters in 2022 to 2023): "Every ad click has a price. A ChatGPT recommendation doesn't." Add a dated,
   sourced number if needed, and keep "isn't for sale" about ChatGPT, where OpenAI says advertisers can't shape,
   rank or alter the answer.
3. **Swap "book an audit" for "paste a product link".** Free audits are the most crowded offer in the category, and all
   of them are gated. GEO's instant, product-level check (real answers, who got picked instead, which sites AI trusts) has
   no equivalent in any competitor ad. Era Search's long-running "10-second test" shows the self-test hook works in AU.
4. **Stay out of the fear-and-hype lane.** "Invisible", "87%", "5.1x", "Rank #1 on ChatGPT", "SEO is dead" and
   agentic-commerce hooks are taken, and most of the numbers are unsourced. Calm, sourced facts and the merchant's own
   data will look different in the feed, and they're safer.
5. **Speak shopper and AU/NZ, not SaaS.** Competitors use B2B examples and jargon (LLM, AEO, GEO). Real shopping
   questions ("gift for a bearded man under $80", "best beard oil for dry skin in Australia") with local prices and
   stores, in plain English, are open ground.
