# Ad facts: "ad costs only go up" (checked 10 Oct 2026)

Ross's angle for the ads: "Ad CPMs only go up and to the right and never come back down. Ranking in AI
answers right now is like free money." This sheet checks that claim against real, citable data.

Every source below was opened and read on 10 Oct 2026. Each fact has: the source URL, the date of the
source, the exact quote, a plain-English copy line (Australian spelling, no em dashes), and caveats.
Same rules as `docs/facts.md`: if a number is not on this sheet (or on `docs/facts.md`), don't put it in an ad.

Rules for using these facts:

- Link every number to its source URL and say the date ("Q2 2026", "2026 report").
- Say where the data comes from. Meta and Alphabet numbers are **global blended averages**. WordStream
  and Tinuiti are **US** campaign data. None of these are Australia-only numbers (see section 6).
- "Average price per ad" (Meta) and "cost-per-click" (Alphabet) are company-wide averages, not the price
  any one store pays. Never present them as "your CPM".
- Never claim GEO will lower anyone's ad costs or replace their ads.

---

## The short answer

- **"Ad costs never come down" is false.** Meta's average price per ad fell year on year for seven
  quarters in a row (Q1 2022 to Q3 2023), by as much as 22%. Google's cost per click also fell year on year
  in 2022 and early 2023. Some industries saw cheaper clicks again in 2026 (beauty search clicks fell 19%).
- **"Ad costs keep rising" is true for the last three years, with dates.** Meta's average price per ad has
  been higher than the year before for 11 quarters in a row (Q4 2023 to Q2 2026), and was up 12% in both
  Q1 and Q2 2026. Google's average cost per click has been higher than the year before in every period
  Alphabet has reported since Q3 2023. The average Google Ads click in WordStream's US benchmark is more
  than twice what it was 10 years ago (US$2.32 in 2016, US$5.42 in 2026), though the 2016 figure came from
  a different sample and method (section 3d).
- **"AI recommendations have no cost per click" is true for the answer itself.** Nobody pays per click for
  the organic recommendation inside a ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity answer. But ChatGPT now shows labelled
  ads below answers, and Google sells ads inside AI Overviews in Australia and New Zealand
  (`docs/facts.md` section 6). And GEO itself costs money.
- **"Like free money" and "CAC is up 222%" should not be used.** See the box at the end.

### What drove the latest rise (Meta's own words)

- **Source:** Meta Form 10-Q, quarter ended 30 June 2026 (URL above), filed July 2026.
- **Quote:** "The increases in average price per ad in the three and six months ended June 30, 2026 were
  driven by an increase in advertising demand, which we believe is mostly due to ongoing improvements to our
  ad performance from our ad targeting and measurement tools, and a favorable foreign currency exchange
  impact. These increases were partially offset by a higher number of ad impressions delivered, especially
  in geographies and in products, such as Reels, that monetize at lower rates."
- **Quote:** "In addition, the online commerce vertical was the largest contributor to the increase in
  advertising revenue in the three and six months ended June 30, 2026 compared to the same periods in 2025."

### Our calculation: where Meta's price per ad sits versus 2021

Chaining Meta's own year-on-year figures (rounded, so approximate):

| Comparison | Change | Working |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 vs Q2 2023 (the low point) | **about +34%** | 1.10 × 1.09 × 1.12 |
| Q2 2026 vs Q2 2021 | **about -3%** | 0.86 × 0.84 × 1.10 × 1.09 × 1.12 |
| Full year 2025 vs full year 2021 | **about -8%** | 0.84 × 0.91 × 1.10 × 1.09 |

So Meta's average price per ad has climbed back to roughly where it was in 2021. It has not gone "up and
to the right" in a straight line.

### Copy lines

- Meta's average price per ad was up 12% on the year before in Q2 2026 (Meta, July 2026).
- Meta's average price per ad has been higher than the year before for 11 quarters in a row (Q4 2023 to Q2 2026).
- Meta says online commerce was the biggest contributor to its ad revenue growth in Q2 2026.

### Caveats

- **The "never" claim is false on Meta's own numbers:** seven straight quarters of falling prices in 2022
  and 2023 (after Apple's 2021 privacy changes and the 2022 ad slowdown).
- It is a **global blended average**: total ad revenue divided by ads shown. It falls when Meta shows more
  ads in cheaper regions or cheaper formats (Reels), and moves with currency. A Shopify brand's own CPM
  can move differently. Don't call it "CPM".
- Meta reports price per ad by region only in charts, not text. There is no Australia-only figure.

## 5. Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

### 5a. The "CAC up 222% in 8 years" stat (SimplicityDX): it doesn't say that

- **Source:** SimplicityDX press release, republished by Marketing Dive:
  https://www.marketingdive.com/press-release/20220720-brands-losing-a-record-29-for-each-new-customer-acquired/
  (original on Business Wire, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220719005425/en/Brands-Losing-a-Record-29-for-Each-New-Customer-Acquired,
  which would not load for me)
- **Date:** 19 to 20 July 2022
- **Headline quote:** "New Research Shows a 222% Increase in Customer Acquisition Costs Puts Renewed Focus on E-Commerce Profitability and Repeat Sales"
- **What the body actually says:** "In 2013, merchants lost on average $9 for every new customer acquired, but today merchants lose $29, a 222% rise in the last eight years. Customer acquisition costs and higher rates of product returns account for virtually all of the difference."
- **Same release, on CAC itself:** "Customer acquisition costs have increased by as much as 60% in the last five years according to industry estimates"
- **Verdict:** the 222% is the rise in the **average loss on a first order** (US$9 to US$29), not in CAC.
  It is a vendor's figure (SimplicityDX sells social commerce software, US and UK) with no published
  method, and it is from 2022. **Do not use "CAC is up 222%".**

### 5b. "CAC up about 60% in five years" (ProfitWell)

- **Source:** https://learn.profitwell.com/blog/how-is-cac-changing-over-time
- **Date:** 13 October 2020
- **Quotes:** "To answer this question, we looked at the data from just under seven hundred subscription companies." and "While B2B CAC is slightly higher, both B2B and B2C are up roughly 60% compared to five years ago."
- **Verdict:** real, but old (2015 to 2020) and about **subscription** companies, not Shopify stores.
  Don't use in ads.

### 5c. Newer 2025 to 2026 CAC studies

I found **no** independent, primary 2025 or 2026 study of DTC or ecommerce CAC. What turns up in search
(for example "CAC up 40% to 60% since 2023", "Shopify Global Commerce Report CAC $274 to $318") comes from
vendor blogs and stats roundups that don't link to a primary source. **Don't use any of them.** The
platform data in sections 1 to 3 is the honest stand-in: the price of the ad is rising, which is the part
we can prove.

## 6. Australia-specific data

### 6a. Spend is at a record (not price)

- **Source:** IAB Australia, https://www.iabaustralia.com.au/news/australian-internet-advertising-market-records-strongest-ever-q1-as-digital-ad-investment-broadens-across-advertiser-base/
  (Internet Advertising Revenue Report, prepared by PwC Australia)
- **Date:** 26 May 2026
- **Quotes:** "The Australian internet advertising market has recorded its strongest ever March quarter, reaching $4.9 billion in Q1 2026" and "total internet advertising expenditure increased 15.3% year-on-year, up from $4.22 billion in Q1 2025" and "Search and Directories remained the largest segment at $2.16 billion, up 13.9% year-on-year."
- **Copy line:** Australian businesses spent a record $4.9 billion on online ads in the March quarter of 2026, up 15% on a year earlier (IAB Australia and PwC).
- **Caveats:** this is **total spend**, not price per click or CPM. More spend can mean more competition,

## 7. Paid ads versus AI recommendations: what the sources really say

### 7a. Nobody has published "cost per order from AI vs from ads"

I found **no** study that compares the cost of a sale from AI recommendations with the cost of a sale from
paid ads. Any ad that says "AI customers are X% cheaper than ad customers" would be made up. What we can
say is simpler: an organic AI recommendation has no cost per click, and the ad click's price is published
(sections 1 to 3).

### 7b. Conversion: the evidence is mixed

- **For:** Adobe (US retail, July 2026), already on `docs/facts.md` 5c:
  https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/08/19/adobe-ai-referral-traffic-data-july-2026/ (19 August 2026).
  Extra quote from the same article: "AI-based retail site visits convert at a rate 60% higher than non-AI
  traffic. That’s the 11th straight month that AI traffic outperformed non-AI traffic in conversion, based
  on the software company’s data." Note: "non-AI traffic" is **all** other traffic, not paid ads.
- **Against:** Kaiser and Schulze, working paper on 973 ecommerce sites, data August 2024 to July 2025.
  - **Source (paper):** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5585812 (written 8 October 2025, posted 18 October 2025, last revised 27 July 2026)
  - **Quote (abstract):** "One year after launch, oLLM exhibits conversion rates and revenue per session above paid social but below all other traditional channels."
  - **Source (coverage):** https://searchengineland.com/llms-google-referral-conversion-study-463747 (Danny Goodwin, 23 October 2025)
  - **Quotes:** "ChatGPT referral traffic was ~0.2% of total sessions" and "ChatGPT trailed paid and organic search on revenue per session, but beat paid social."
- **Verdict:** don't say "AI shoppers convert better than ad clicks". It depends on the data set and the
  year. The Adobe line, labelled US and dated, is fine. Also: in 2024 to 2025 AI was a **small** share of
  store traffic (about 0.2% of sessions in that study), so never promise it will replace ads.

### 7c. Is there a cost per click for AI answers?

- **The answer itself: no.** OpenAI: "advertisers have no ability to shape, rank, or alter ChatGPT’s
  responses" (`docs/facts.md` section 6).
- **But there are paid slots around it:** ChatGPT ads (labelled, below the answer, Free and Go plans,
  live in Australia and New Zealand) and Google ads "above, below or within AI Overviews" in Australia and
  New Zealand (`docs/facts.md` section 6).
- ChatGPT ad prices: trade press repeats a figure of about US$60 per 1,000 views, traced to paywalled
  reporting by The Information that I could not open. **Not verified. Don't use.**
- Ads in Google's AI Mode: I could not confirm they run in Australia. Don't claim either way.
- **New ad format (5 Oct 2026):** OpenAI is adding visual ads alongside images ChatGPT generates. TechCrunch:
  "The new ads will begin to appear later this month in the U.S. only for now [...] The ads will also be
  clearly labeled and won't influence the answers ChatGPT provides"
  (https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-launches-visual-ads-that-appear-alongside-image-generation-results/,
  read 10 Oct 2026). So don't describe ChatGPT ads as only "below the answer"; use OpenAI's "always clearly
  labeled and separate from ChatGPT's answers" (23 Sep 2026), and re-check weekly.

### 7d. Does ChatGPT always add utm_source=chatgpt.com? No.

- **Source:** Seer Interactive, https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/are-ai-sites-like-chatgpt-sending-your-website-traffic
  ("originally published on December 2, 2024 and was updated on June 22, 2026"; read 10 Oct 2026)
- **Quotes:** "sources are certainly the most likely scenario where UTMs are supplied ... but just because a
  link is supplied as a source does not guarantee UTMs will always be included" (Seer's dash cut with "..."); its
  table shows ChatGPT "Search Results" with UTMs "❌"; "We'd encourage you to treat any AI traffic number as
  a directional floor rather than a precise count."
- **Our own answers (10 Oct 2026, `docs/ads/evidence/`):** the tag was on the links in 2 of 8 saved ChatGPT
  answers (as captured by the scraper).
- **Copy line:** "ChatGPT often adds a little tag to its links. We count the AI orders we can trace."

### 7e. Paid and organic ChatGPT traffic can't yet be split cleanly

- **Same Seer page:** "We haven't yet nailed down a clean, reliable way to separate the two inside GA4" and
  "treat any AI Assistant or AI-referral numbers in your reports as a blend of both organic and paid until
  you've done some manual spot-checking of your own campaigns and UTM conventions."
- ChatGPT ads are live in Australia (confirmed in OpenAI's 23 Sep 2026 post, `docs/facts.md` section 6). GEO's classifier
  (`app/lib/attribution.ts`, `classifyVisit`) counts any chatgpt.com referrer or chatgpt `utm_source` as
  AI and doesn't yet use `utm_medium`. **Until paid clicks are split out, no ad may imply every counted AI
  order was unpaid.**

## Safe ways to say it / Don't say

> ### Verdicts on the three claims
>
> | Claim | Verdict | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | "Ad costs never come down" | **FALSE. Don't say it.** | Meta's price per ad fell for 7 quarters in a row (2022 to 2023, down to -22%). Google's CPC fell in 2022 and early 2023. Beauty search clicks fell 19% in 2026. Facebook CPMs fell 4% in Q1 2026 (Tinuiti). Meta's Q2 2026 price per ad is about where it was in Q2 2021. |
> | "Ad costs keep rising" | **TRUE if dated.** | Meta: higher than the year before for 11 quarters straight (Q4 2023 to Q2 2026), +12% in Q2 2026. Google: higher than the year before in every period reported since Q3 2023. WordStream: average US click more than doubled since 2016. Always add the period and source. |
> | "AI recommendations have no cost per click" | **TRUE for the organic answer.** | You don't pay per click when ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity names your product in the answer. But ChatGPT shows labelled ads below answers, and Google sells ads inside AI Overviews in AU/NZ. Getting recommended takes work, and GEO costs US$97 a month. |
>
> ### Safe ways to say it
>
> - "Meta's average price per ad was 12% higher than a year earlier (Q2 2026)." *(Meta, July 2026)*
> - "Ad prices on Facebook and Instagram have been higher than the year before for 11 quarters in a row." *(Meta, Q4 2023 to Q2 2026)*
> - "Average Google Ads click in the US: US$2.32 (2016), US$5.42 (2026)." *(WordStream benchmarks, US search campaigns; 2016 figure from its first report, methods differ; not adjusted for inflation)*
> - "A Google Ads click for shopping and gifts went from US$2.61 to US$4.14 in two years." *(WordStream, US)*
> - "When ChatGPT recommends your product, there's no cost per click." / "No ad spend. No cost per click."
> - "Ads get a label and a price. The recommendation in the answer is earned." *(OpenAI says advertisers can't change ChatGPT's answers)*
> - "Ad prices do go up and down. Since late 2023, they've mostly gone up." *(fair summary of sections 1 to 3)*
> - Pair them, so readers connect the dots: "Meta: +12% per ad on last year. A ChatGPT recommendation: no cost per click."
>
> ### Don't say
>
> - "Ad costs never come down" / "CPMs only ever go up" / "always up and to the right" (false, see above).
> - "Ad costs are higher than ever" for Meta (its average price per ad is roughly back at its 2021 level).
> - "CAC is up 222%" (that number is the loss per new customer, from a 2022 vendor release).
> - "CAC is up 60%" (2020 data, subscription companies).
> - "Australian ad costs are rising X%" (no reliable public data; the IAB number is total spend, not price).
> - "Your CPM", "your cost per click", or any number presented as what the reader pays.
> - "Ranking in AI is free money" / "free traffic forever" (hype, implies a guaranteed return; AI is still a small share of store traffic and GEO costs US$97 a month).
> - "GEO is free" (the recommendation has no cost per click; GEO itself is a paid app).
> - "AI shoppers convert better than ad clicks" or "AI customers are cheaper than ad customers" (no source shows this; one academic study found the opposite for paid search in 2024 to 2025).
> - "AI assistants have no ads" / "you can't pay to appear in AI answers" (ChatGPT has ads below answers; Google sells ads inside AI Overviews in AU/NZ).
> - "GEO will lower your ad costs" or "replace your ads".
> - Any ChatGPT ad price (the US$60 CPM figure is unverified).
