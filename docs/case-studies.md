# Case studies: brands that got sales from AI answers

Research for the GEO home page (https://usegeo.app). Compiled 12 October 2026.

**How to use these.** Every case below is another company's result, reported by that company or by the press about them. None of them used GEO. On the site, show each one as "Reported by [publisher]" with a link to the article, and use the brand's own numbers word for word. Do not round, combine or restate them as our results.

**How they were checked.** Every article listed here was opened and read in full (through a page reader, because direct web access is blocked from the build machine). Each quote is copied from the article text. Each link loaded and returned the article at the URL given. Paywalls are noted where they apply.

**Ranking.** Ordered by how well each would work on a Shopify store's home page: online store first, then named brand, then a clear sales result, then a trustworthy source.

## 1. Viv (period care, online store)

- **Business:** DTC period care brand (tampons, pads). Sells on its own Shopify store.
- **Result in plain words:** Shoppers sent to Viv's blog by ChatGPT bought products, and sales from those recommendations rose 436%. Viv also sold out of about six months of tampon stock in three weeks, which the brand credits to Google's AI recommendations.
- **Exact quotes:**
  - "What’s more, many of the people who were directed to Viv’s blog by ChatGPT ended up buying products. Sales converted from these recommendations jumped 436%." (the reporter's words, based on Viv's figures)
  - “We ended up selling out of about six months of tampon inventory in three weeks, driven by Google’s AI-powered recommendations,” Donohue said.
- **Who said it:** Kelly Donohue, marketing and design director at Viv.
- **Article:** "Brands see an influx of traffic from ChatGPT and Google Gemini"
- **Publisher / author:** Modern Retail (Digiday Media), by Gabriela Barkho
- **Date:** 14 January 2025 (describes events from July 2024 onwards)
- **URL:** https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/brands-are-seeing-an-influx-of-traffic-from-chatgpt-and-google-gemini/
- **Free-to-read backup:** Search Engine Journal retells the same story with the same quotes: https://www.searchenginejournal.com/are-people-clicking-links-in-chatgpt-search-brands-say-yes/537954/
- **Ecommerce:** Yes
- **Trust:** High. A respected retail trade publication, with a named executive on the record. Modern Retail gives 3 free articles a month, then asks readers to subscribe.

## 2. Draco Diamond (lab-grown diamond jewellery, online store)

- **Business:** Canada-based online seller of made-to-order lab-grown diamond jewellery.
- **Result in plain words:** After the founder worked on showing up in AI answers in spring 2026, AI tools went from 0.18% to 5.45% of the site's traffic. Revenue was up 78% year on year in June and 195% in July. Shoppers from AI added items to their cart five times as often as Google Ads visitors.
- **Exact quotes:**
  - "Draco Diamond’s share of traffic from AI answer engines increased to 5.45% after McMartin’s AEO push, from 0.18% before."
  - "McMartin also spotted a correlation between his AEO push and Draco Diamond’s year-over-year revenue increases. In April and May 2026, respectively, Draco Diamond revenue rose 5% and 10% compared to the same months in 2025. Then, in June and July, after he had completed that push, revenue grew 78% and 195% year over year in June and July, respectively."
  - "McMartin shared that consumers who arrive at Draco Diamond’s website from AI sources add items to their cart five times as often as Google Ads visitors, and at more than double the rate of the site average."
- **Who said it:** Garrett McMartin, founder.
- **Article:** "Draco Diamond credits AEO, AI traffic trends ahead of Cyber 5"
- **Publisher / author:** Digital Commerce 360, by Abbas Haleem
- **Date:** 26 August 2026
- **URL:** https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/08/26/draco-diamond-aeo-ai-traffic-cyber-5/
- **Ecommerce:** Yes
- **Trust:** High for the source (a long-running ecommerce trade publication, founder on the record). The article itself calls the revenue jump a "correlation", so on the site say "revenue grew 78% and 195% after", not "because of".

- **Article:** "How SteelSeries claimed the top AI search spot before competitors arrived" (Gold in Search, The Drum Awards for Marketing)
- **Publisher:** The Drum (awards case study)
- **Date:** 5 June 2026

## Context stats (industry-wide, not case studies)

1. **Shopify, Q1 2026 platform data.** "AI-referred sessions convert at nearly 50% higher rates than organic search." "Average order values from AI-referred sessions are 14% higher than organic search." "AI-referred orders on Shopify grew nearly 13x year-over-year in Q1 2026." Shopify also notes that Google AI Overviews visits are counted as normal search, so the real AI share is likely higher. Source: "AI-referred shoppers convert better and spend more: What Shopify's early data shows", Shopify Enterprise blog, by Kyle Risley, 11 May 2026. https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/ai-search-insights. Trust: high (first-party platform data).
2. **Adobe, US retail, July 2026.** "Adobe found that shoppers using AI referrals generate 53% more revenue per visit than other shoppers. Additionally, AI-based retail site visits convert at a rate 60% higher than non-AI traffic." Source: "Adobe: AI-referral traffic spending, converting more than counterparts", Digital Commerce 360 reporting Adobe Analytics, 19 August 2026. https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/08/19/adobe-ai-referral-traffic-data-july-2026/. Trust: high (based on over 1 trillion visits to US retail sites).
3. **Counterpoint: academic study of 973 online stores (August 2024 to July 2025).** "ChatGPT referral traffic converts worse than Google search, email and affiliate links, trailing on both conversion rate and revenue per session, according to a new analysis of 973 ecommerce sites." It also found ChatGPT was "~0.2% of total sessions" and that its conversion rate was improving. Source: "ChatGPT, LLM referrals convert worse than Google Search: Study", Search Engine Land, by Danny Goodwin, 23 October 2025 (date from a syndicated copy; the page itself showed none). https://searchengineland.com/llms-google-referral-conversion-study-463747. Trust: high. Worth knowing so the home page claims stay honest: newer data (Shopify, Adobe) is more positive, but results vary by store.
